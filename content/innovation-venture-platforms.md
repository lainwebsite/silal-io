## 3. Farm Innovation Fund (FIF)

Source: `Farm_Innovation_Fund_General.pptx` ("General overview"), data from the FIF Steering Committee presentation, **15 September 2026**.

- **Tagline:** *A practical pathway from farm needs to tested solutions.* (cover + closing slide; "Innovation Oasis by Silal")
- **Audience line (closing slide):** For farmers, technology providers, research partners and public-sector stakeholders.
- **Vision — Farmers drive every stage:** The Farm Innovation Fund connects real farm problems with a structured path to develop, test and scale practical solutions.
  - Central statement: **Enable farmers across the UAE to improve farm performance and profitability through on-farm innovation.**
  - Ideas can enter at different levels of maturity.
  - The programme adapts its support to the evidence, readiness and needs of each proposal.
  - Farmers keep ownership of their ideas at every stage.
  - Value is measured beyond one competition cycle.

**Who the fund supports** — open to farmers and farm operators across the UAE, whatever the maturity of the idea:
1. **UAE farmers and farm operators** — a clear operational challenge or an opportunity to improve production.
2. **Ideas at any stage** — early concepts and solutions that are ready for an on-farm trial can both enter the pathway.
3. **Pilots with measurable value** — proposals develop clear evidence, feasibility, cost, data and adoption plans.
> Current cycle: five pilots may receive up to AED 200,000 each.

**Four areas of farm innovation** (categories used in application and evaluation):
| Area | Covers |
|---|---|
| Protected Agriculture & Greenhouses | Structures, climate control and crop production |
| Sustainable & Organic Practices | Soils, inputs, biological solutions and circularity |
| Water & Irrigation Management | Water quality, efficiency, monitoring and reuse |
| Smart Farming & Digital Technology | Sensors, automation, analytics and farm decisions |

**Process — From farm need to field evidence** (a staged process strengthens ideas before funding and keeps farmer ownership at the centre):
1. **Discovery & Call** — farmers submit a problem and a proposed solution.
2. **Eligibility** — the team checks completeness and programme fit.
3. **Training & Proposal** — workshops and mentoring strengthen the pilot design.
4. **Review & Pitches** — experts assess the evidence and finalists present.
5. **Pilot & Scale** — selected teams trial, measure and consider adoption.
> Each gate adds evidence before the programme commits pilot funding and resources.

**Cohort 1 at a glance** (as of 15 Sep 2026):
| 2,000+ | 130 | 68 | 39 | 10 | 7 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Farms reached | Applications submitted | Advanced to Phase 2 | Valid Phase 2 proposals | Finalists | Emirates represented |

Current cycle target: **5 pilots leading to at least 1 scaled solution.**

**Innovation Day — stronger pilot proposals:** 26 Jul 2026, full-day event at Innovation Oasis, Al Ain: facility tour, workshops and direct mentoring. **38 farmers**, **3 workshops** (Arabic facilitation with practical workbooks). Photos in the deck: farmers in the IO "AGRITECH" room, lab visit, group shot in the atrium.

**What selected pilot teams receive** (proposal → funded, measured on-farm pilot with a defined route to adoption):
1. **Pilot funding** — up to AED 200,000 per selected pilot in the current cycle.
2. **Pilot readiness** — a defined scope, baseline, site, season and resource plan.
3. **Delivery discipline** — milestones, data logging, success KPIs and review points.
4. **Visibility and scale** — a close-out review, farmer demonstration and adoption pathway.

**Value beyond one competition:** 5 pilots funded in the current cycle · **≥ 1 solution scaled across the ecosystem (North Star)**. Each pilot is expected to deliver:
- **Farm value** — performance, efficiency or profitability measured during the pilot.
- **Evidence** — comparable data and practical lessons that other farms can use.
- **Adoption** — a clear next step for farmers, funders and implementation partners.

Gaps: application link/form, cycle dates for cycle 2, eligibility rules, Arabic copy (audience is Arabic-first: Innovation Day ran in Arabic), farmer stories of the 10 finalists.

## 4. Incubation (iO Incubator)

Source: `Incubation_MarCom_Brief_202604.docx` (requested 27 Apr 2026, Department: Innovation Oasis). **Internal brief:** facts below are fine for the site; the budget, the risk list and the internal alignment notes are not (§8).

- **Programme:** iO Incubator — *a platform designed for early-staged ventures.*
- **Inaugural cohort:** **Agri-Robotics Cohort 2026**, a collaboration between **Silal and Khalifa University**, designed to accelerate the commercial readiness of four agricultural technologies.
- **Timeline:** Nov 2025 – Nov 2026. Announcement planned for 5 May 2026 (tentative) at **Make it in the Emirates**.
- **Stakeholders:** Silal Group, Khalifa University.
- Speaker notes (PR deck) place it at **TRL 3** ("incubate early-stage lab research"), the Accelerator at immediate commercial application.

**Cohort 1 technologies:**
| Name | What it is | Aim |
|---|---|---|
| **TouchRIPE** | Wearable device; non-destructive fruit-firmness assessment with vision-based tactile sensors | Objective ripeness evaluation, better shelf-life forecasting, less food waste, higher marketable yields (post-harvest market) |
| **PollenMatic** | Compact, versatile device for efficient pollination of greenhouse tomatoes | Refine the prototype and validate in real conditions to **TRL 6 / CRL 5**; cost-effective, user-friendly artificial pollination |
| **PlantAI** | AI mobile app detecting leaf diseases in key UAE crops (tomatoes, cucumbers, capsicum, berries) | Validate accuracy across field environments, reach commercial readiness; earlier detection, timely intervention |
| **AerialYield** | Autonomous system for yield estimation and ripeness analysis in blueberry cultivation | Real-world data, field trials, end-user validation; better forecasting, less labour |

**Objectives:**
1. Develop cutting-edge technologies to address key challenges in UAE agriculture.
2. Advance prototypes to market-ready solutions aligned with industry standards and market demands.
3. Promote efficient, sustainable farming: less waste, better yields, better resource use.
4. Combine Khalifa University and Silal expertise; introduce locally developed solutions, reduce reliance on imports, grow the UAE agri-tech market.

**Why it matters (significance):** closes the commercialisation gap between research and the market · tackles farm-level challenges (less labour dependency, better farm performance, less waste-related loss) · strengthens the local ecosystem and gives academia industry access.

**Differentiators (use on the page):**
1. **Integrated infrastructure and testbeds** — open access to greenhouses, open fields, labs and real farm operations.
2. **Operator exposure** — project teams work directly with end users (farmers): practical and scientifically rigorous.
3. **Built for UAE constraints** — designed around local regulatory, environmental and market realities, not global solutions adapted afterwards.

**Success measure:** at least 1 project converted into new jointly-owned IP, a commercial or scaled deployment, or further investment; each project assessed on measurable gains in yield, resource efficiency or cost vs. baseline.

## 6. Agricultural Challenges — Al Miyah Challenge for Agriculture

Source: `Al_Miyah_Challenge_Silal_Technical_Partner.pptx` (facts from mohamedbinzayedwi.ae, Abu Dhabi Media Office, The National, Apr 2026). Deck subtitle: *Executing five field pilots and building the CRETA greenhouse at Innovation Oasis, Al Ain.*

**The challenge:** inaugural competition of the **Mohamed bin Zayed Water Initiative (MBZWI)**; rewards technologies that cut water use in farming without giving up yield.
- Agriculture uses **nearly 70%** of all water consumed globally.
- Open to innovators, companies, start-ups and researchers worldwide; solutions must be proven and ready to pilot in the UAE.
- **AED 8 million** prize pool (about AED 4 million for first place). Results must transfer to similar climates: the GCC and Sub-Saharan Africa.
- **Problem Statement A — Sustainable irrigation** (reduce overall water consumption). **Problem Statement B — Controlled-environment design** (greenhouses and net houses, incl. water for cooling).
- Technology categories: water consumption reduction (indoor/outdoor) · alternate water supply · water quality treatment · soil and crop modification · circular solutions combining components.

**At a glance:** **846** teams registered · **54** countries · **21** qualifying teams from 13 countries · **6** finalists in real-world testing · **AED 8M** prize pool · **12** months of testing in the UAE. Next milestone: winners and runners-up announced **December 2026** after testing at IO.

**From 846 entries to six finalists:**
1. Apr–Jun 2025 · Open call — 846 teams from 54 countries (deadline 30 Jun 2025).
2. Sep 2025 · Qualifying — 21 teams from 13 countries; pitches and site visits.
3. Nov 2025 · Testing — indoor and outdoor trials begin at Innovation Oasis, Al Ain, up to 12 months.
4. Apr 2026 · Finalists — six confirmed: two from the UAE, one each from Egypt, Brazil, Argentina, India.
5. Dec 2026 · Winners — first place includes further testing and deployment in the UAE.
> Silal enters at stage 3: every finalist technology is tested on Silal ground, under UAE conditions, before the jury decides.

**Partners and roles:** Organiser **MBZWI** (designs and runs the challenge, funds the prize, convenes the jury) · **Zayed for Good Foundation** (strategic partner) · **ADAFSA** (oversight, technical expertise, evaluation under local climatic conditions) · **ASPIRE** (innovation acceleration) · **Technical partner: Silal** (hosts and executes the trials at IO: five field pilots and the CRETA greenhouse build). Silal quote: *"Water scarcity is a critical challenge for agriculture in the UAE, and tackling it requires innovation and collaboration."*

**What Silal / IO brings:** **The site** (greenhouses, nethouses, open-field plots, labs on one campus) · **The execution** (trial design, crop management, instrumentation, data capture over 12 months) · **The evidence** (water use, yield, performance under UAE conditions, evaluated with ADAFSA).

**Five field pilots (Problem A, one common protocol: same crops, sites and irrigation baselines):**
| Country | Finalist | Solution |
|---|---|---|
| UAE | **Raincatcher** | Material beneath the root zone absorbs irrigation water and releases it back to roots, cutting evaporation and deep-drainage losses |
| India | **EF Polymer** | Biodegradable superabsorbent polymer from fruit waste holds water and nutrients between irrigations |
| Argentina | **M4LIFE** | Custom microbes help soil retain water and release it gradually; drought and salinity support |
| Brazil | **Krilltech** | Natural bio-stimulant with carbon nanoparticles improves plant water-use efficiency |
| Egypt | **Agri-Vit** | Foliar biostimulant strengthens resilience to heat and salinity with less irrigation |
