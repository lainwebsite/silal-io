From lab research to market-ready. A platform for early-stage ventures.

The IO Incubator’s inaugural cohort, Agri-Robotics 2026, is a collaboration between Silal and Khalifa University to accelerate the commercial readiness of four agricultural technologies.

Teams refine prototypes with open access to greenhouses, open fields, labs and real farm operations, working directly with the farmers who will use them. The goal is to close the commercialisation gap between research and the market with solutions built for local conditions.

How is this platform different from other programs?

It is for growth-stage startups with proven technology, not early-stage teams. Cohorts are small (3 to 5 startups, two cohorts a year), so each startup gets a tailored pilot. Pilots run inside Silal’s actual operating environment with real business units, not in a sandbox, and successful pilots lead to defined commercial pathways, not just a demo day.

Does participation require giving up equity?

No. Silal does not take equity as a condition of participation. Selected participants enter into a Participant Agreement with commercial provisions in Silal’s favour, typically a Right of First Refusal on GCC commercial deployment for a defined period post-pilot, revenue participation on partnerships Silal originates, and first-conversation rights on GCC opportunities. Participants retain ownership and global commercial freedom outside the GCC.

Do startups need to be based in the UAE?

No. We prioritize companies committed to building a meaningful presence in the UAE and broader GCC region. Selected startups spend time on-site at Innovation Oasis in Al Ain during the pilot phase, working closely with Silal teams and partners.

What is the expected time commitment?

A structured engagement over 3 to 5 months, combining in-person time and ongoing collaboration with Silal teams, with time upfront to scope and define the pilot.

Is a pilot or commercial engagement guaranteed?

The platform is designed to enable pilots and commercial opportunities, but outcomes are not guaranteed. Where pilots are successful, Silal will work with startups to explore next steps, including potential commercial agreements or longer-term partnerships.

What language is used?

Please submit all application materials in English. The program is conducted in English; no Arabic is required.

Al Miyah Challenge for Agriculture

Organised by the Mohamed bin Zayed Water Initiative · Silal as technical partner

Testing at Innovation Oasis · winners December 2026

Agriculture uses nearly 70% of all water consumed globally. The challenge rewards solutions that cut water use while maintaining or improving yield.

The inaugural competition of the Mohamed bin Zayed Water Initiative is open to innovators, companies, start-ups and researchers worldwide. Solutions must be proven and ready to be piloted in the UAE, with results that transfer to similar climates in the GCC and Sub-Saharan Africa.

AED 8 million prize pool, about AED 4 million for first place.

Sustainable irrigation

Improving sustainable irrigation practices and reducing overall water consumption in agriculture.

Problem statement B · CRETA Group, UAE

A greenhouse cooled by the ground.

Hot desert air is drawn through underground channels, where stable soil temperatures pre-condition it before it enters the growing space. The dual-shell structure reduces the heat load with minimal energy demand.

12–15°Creported reduction in pilot testing
Zerowater used for cooling

Silal builds and commissions it at Innovation Oasis, runs a full crop cycle benchmarked against a conventional evaporative-cooled greenhouse, and shares the operating data with ADAFSA.

What the testing phase measures

Less water, same or better yield, ready to scale.

  • Water use

    Irrigation volume per crop cycle and per kilogram of produce, against an untreated control.

  • Yield and quality

    Harvested weight, marketable grade and crop health under heat and salinity.

  • Operating performance

    Energy demand, labour and input cost; for the greenhouse, conditions through the summer peak.

  • Scalability

    Cost per hectare, ease of adoption on UAE farms and transfer to similar climates.
