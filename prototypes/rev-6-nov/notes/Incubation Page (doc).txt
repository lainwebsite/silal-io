2. What Incubation Means at IO

OUR MODEL

Not open-ended research. Not a conventional accelerator.

IO Incubation is a tech translation model for determining the fastest credible pathway from research or prototype to first user.

Each project is structured as a sequence of focused experiments. Rather than committing immediately to a long development roadmap, IO and the project team identify the most consequential uncertainty, design the smallest credible test to resolve it and use the evidence to determine what happens next.

Three defining principles

Start with the problem

Projects must address an evidenced and strategically relevant need, with a defined user, customer or problem owner.

Invest in learning

Every activity should generate evidence that reduces uncertainty or unlocks a specific next decision.

Follow the evidence

Projects may continue, pivot or stop. Negative results are valuable when they prevent unnecessary development or reveal a stronger route forward.

Incubation is successful when it produces faster, data-driven decision making, not simply when a project completes its original roadmap.

3. Who Incubation Is For

WHO IT IS FOR

Promising opportunities that need focused validation

IO Incubation supports researchers, universities, scientific teams, technology developers and early-stage ventures with promising solutions that require further evidence before they can progress.

Suitable opportunities may begin as research, intellectual property, an early prototype or an early-stage venture proposition. What matters is that the opportunity addresses a clearly defined agrifood need and can benefit from focused technical, user, operational or commercial validation.

Research teams and universities

Translate scientific research, intellectual property or laboratory findings into a defined product, service, capability or commercial application.

Early-stage ventures

Test the critical assumptions behind an emerging venture, strengthen the product and value proposition, and generate the evidence needed to identify a credible route toward customers, partnerships or further investment.

Technology developers

Validate an early prototype, adapt an existing solution for agrifood or UAE conditions, or resolve critical technical, operational and market uncertainties.

Collaborative teams

Bring together research, engineering, agronomy, product, commercial and industry expertise to develop and validate a shared opportunity.

Entry Profile

Incubation is most relevant where:

-   The opportunity addresses a clearly evidenced agrifood problem

-   The intended user, customer or problem owner can be identified

-   An early solution, prototype, research output or technical asset already exists

-   Important technical, customer, operational or commercial assumptions remain unvalidated

-   A focused experiment could resolve a critical uncertainty or unlock a defined next step

-   The core team has the capability and commitment to participate in the validation process

-   IO can provide meaningful access to expertise, facilities, data, users, operating environments or commercial relationships

-   There is an initial hypothesis for how the opportunity could ultimately create value

Incubation is very selective. Opportunities are assessed based on the importance of the problem, strength of existing evidence, team capability, potential value and fit with IO’s capabilities.

4. Where Incubation Sits

MEASURING READINESS

From early proof of concept to real-world validation

Technology Readiness Levels, or TRLs, show how far a technology has progressed from an initial idea toward proven operational use.

IO Incubation typically supports technologies between TRL 3 and TRL 6, helping teams move from experimental proof of concept toward validation in relevant conditions.

Technology Readiness Levels

-   TRL 1–2: Early research and technology concept

-   TRL 3: Experimental proof of concept

-   TRL 4: Technology validated in the laboratory

-   TRL 5: Technology validated in a relevant environment

-   TRL 6: Technology demonstrated in a relevant environment

-   TRL 7: Prototype demonstrated in an operational environment

-   TRL 8–9: Complete solution proven through operational use

IO Incubation

Typically TRL 3–6

For research, prototypes and early-stage ventures that still require focused technical, user or commercial validation.

Acceleration

Typically TRL 7 and above

For mature technologies ready for operational validation, localization and commercial engagement.

These ranges are indicative. The right pathway also depends on the evidence available and the questions that still need to be answered.

5. How Incubation Works

THE INCUBATION JOURNEY

Evidence first. Investment follows.

Each incubation project follows a tailored sequence of learning cycles. The scope, timeline and support model depend on the opportunity, the evidence already available and the most consequential uncertainties.

01. Frame the opportunity

Define:

-   The problem

-   The intended user or customer

-   The current baseline

-   Existing alternatives

-   The strategic importance

-   The initial solution and value hypothesis

Output

A clearly framed opportunity and initial route-to-value hypothesis

02. Assess the evidence

Review:

-   Existing technical results

-   User and customer evidence

-   Prior art and competing solutions

-   Intellectual-property position

-   Team capability

-   Market and adoption assumptions

-   Regulatory and operational barriers

Output

A clear picture of what is known, unknown and assumed

03. Prioritize the critical uncertainty

Identify the assumption most likely to determine whether the opportunity should progress.

Examples include:

-   Technical performance

-   User need

-   Customer willingness to pay

-   Unit economics

-   Operational adoption

-   Manufacturability

-   Regulatory feasibility

-   Intellectual-property freedom to operate

Output

A prioritized validation question and decision gate

04. Design the experiment

Develop the smallest credible experiment capable of resolving the priority uncertainty.

This may involve:

-   Laboratory analysis

-   Prototype iteration

-   Greenhouse or field testing

-   User interviews

-   Workflow testing

-   Market testing

-   Cost modelling

-   Prior-art or IP assessment

Output

An agreed experiment, evidence plan and success criteria

05. Run the validation cycle

Implement the experiment with access to the required expertise, facilities, users, data and operating environments.

Output

Technical, user, operational or commercial evidence

06. Review and decide

Assess the results against predefined criteria.

Every cycle concludes with one of three decisions:

Continue

The evidence supports another focused validation cycle.

Pivot

The evidence suggests that the use case, customer, product design, technical approach or value pathway should change.

Stop

The evidence no longer justifies further investment, while useful data, know-how or intellectual property are documented and preserved.

Output

An evidence-based decision and next-step recommendation

7. What Teams Commit

ACCOUNTABLE OWNERSHIP

Strong projects need capable, committed teams

Incubation is a collaborative development process. Project teams remain accountable for the technical work, project delivery and quality of evidence.

Teams should be prepared to:

-   Assign a capable and accountable project lead

-   Commit the required scientific and technical contributors

-   Participate in customer and user discovery

-   Share relevant data, research and project information

-   Respond to evidence, including changing the proposed approach

-   Work against defined decision gates and deadlines

-   Clarify ownership, background IP and new IP

-   Support project reporting and governance

-   Engage with relevant users, partners or sponsors

-   Accept that incubation may recommend a pivot or stop decision

Suggested callout

Incubation is not designed to protect the original idea. It is designed to find the strongest evidence-supported pathway to value.

8. How Opportunities Are Selected

SELECTION PRINCIPLES

Where IO can make a meaningful difference

Projects are not selected only because the technology is interesting. IO prioritizes opportunities where the problem matters, the potential value is credible and IO can provide a differentiated advantage.

01. Problem relevance

Is the project addressing an evidenced and strategically important agrifood challenge?

02. Clear user or customer

Is there an identified problem owner, user, customer or operational sponsor?

03. Strength of early evidence

Is there sufficient technical or user evidence to justify further validation?

04. IO’s right to contribute

Can IO provide important access to farms, facilities, data, expertise, customers, regulators or operating environments?

05. Team capability

Is the required scientific, technical, product and commercial capability available and accountable?

06. Potential value

Could the opportunity create meaningful operational, commercial, strategic or intellectual-property value more than will be invested to make it successful?

07. Evidence gap

Can a focused experiment resolve a defined uncertainty or unlock a specific next step?

08. Route beyond incubation

Is there an initial hypothesis for deployment, licensing, partnership, investment or another value pathway?

11. What Success Looks Like

INCUBATION OUTCOMES

Better evidence. Better decisions. Stronger pathways.

Success is not defined only by advancing a technology-readiness level or completing a planned list of deliverables.

IO measures incubation through the quality and speed of learning and the extent to which evidence unlocks a credible next step.

Learning

-   Critical assumptions tested

-   Validation cycles completed

-   Technical and commercial uncertainties resolved

-   Evidence-based pivots made

-   Resources redirected away from weak pathways

Validation

-   Performance demonstrated against a relevant baseline

-   User or customer need confirmed

-   Operational conditions understood

-   Unit economics or value case strengthened

-   Adoption barriers identified and addressed

Assets created

-   Locally relevant data

-   Validated protocols

-   Prototype improvements

-   Defensible know-how

-   Intellectual-property opportunities

-   Reusable technical or market insight

Pathways unlocked

-   Internal deployment

-   Licensing

-   Co-development

-   Procurement

-   Strategic partnership

-   Follow-on investment

-   Venture creation

-   Progression to later-stage validation

Recommended success statement

Incubation succeeds when a project generates enough evidence to support a confident decision and a credible pathway forward, including when that decision is to pivot or stop.

The current design states that success means at least one project becomes jointly owned IP, a commercial or scaled deployment, or attracts further investment. That can remain as an aspirational portfolio objective, but it is too narrow as the sole definition of success. It excludes valuable no-go decisions, reusable data, validated learning and strategic capability. [Incubation...rview.pptx | PowerPoint]
