2. The Why

Eyebrow

WHY THE FUND

Supporting local farmers to innovate, grow and build resilience

Introductory copy

Farmers understand their operational challenges better than anyone. They often also have practical ideas for improving how farms use water, manage crops, reduce costs, increase yields or adapt to changing conditions.

However, turning an idea into a credible project can require funding, specialist expertise and a structured way to test whether the solution works.

The Farm Innovation Fund helps bridge that gap.

Three purpose pillars

Improve productivity

Support solutions that improve yields, quality, efficiency and day-to-day farm performance.

Strengthen profitability

Help farms reduce costs, improve returns and identify new sources of value.

Build resilience

Enable farms to adapt to resource constraints, environmental pressures and changing production needs.

Design guidance

-   Replace the current fragmented introductory text with one clear purpose statement and three supporting pillars.

-   Use productivity, profitability and resilience as visually prominent words.

-   Keep the section succinct. The objective is to establish the Fund’s purpose before explaining eligibility or process.

4. Who Can Participate

Eyebrow

WHO CAN PARTICIPATE

Supporting local ideas for better farming

The Farm Innovation Fund is designed for UAE National farmers, farm owners and idea sponsors who want to address a practical challenge or opportunity in agriculture.

Applicants may bring forward a challenge experienced on their own farm or sponsor an idea with potential value for UAE agriculture. They can also involve farm managers, entrepreneurs, researchers, technology providers and technical specialists to help develop and deliver the project.

Participation Models

Farmers and farm owners

Identify and test practical ideas that could improve farm productivity, reduce costs, increase profitability, use resources more efficiently or strengthen resilience.

Idea sponsors

Bring forward or sponsor an idea that responds to a relevant agricultural need, while operational or technical partners support its development and delivery.

Collaborative teams

Applicants may work with farm managers, entrepreneurs, researchers, technology providers or other specialists to develop and test the solution.

Eligibility Callout

Each application must be led or sponsored by a UAE National and address a clearly defined on-farm or agricultural challenge relevant to the UAE.

Applicants do not need to have every technical capability in place before applying. The Fund can help bring together the expertise, facilities and partners required to develop and test a promising idea.

5. What Ideas Can the Fund Support?

KEY FOCUS AREAS

Practical innovation for better farm performance

The following are common thematic areas we see addressing the local farming challenges

01. Water and Irrigation Management

Use water more intelligently and efficiently

Ideas that help farms optimize water use, improve irrigation performance, manage water quality or strengthen resilience to water scarcity and salinity.

Examples may include:

-   Precision irrigation and automated scheduling

-   Soil-moisture and crop-water monitoring

-   Irrigation-system optimization

-   Leak detection and reduction of water losses

-   Water treatment, recycling and reuse

-   Salinity monitoring and management

-   Alternative water sources

-   Data-led irrigation decisions

Example farmer challenge

How might a farm reduce water consumption without compromising crop yield or quality?

02. Protected Agriculture

Improve growing conditions, efficiency and crop performance

Ideas that strengthen production within greenhouses, nethouses and other protected growing environments, particularly under challenging climatic conditions.

Examples may include:

-   Greenhouse cooling and climate control

-   Energy-efficient growing systems

-   Environmental monitoring and automation

-   Ventilation, humidity and airflow management

-   Improved greenhouse or nethouse designs

-   Hydroponic and soilless production systems

-   Lighting and shading solutions

-   Improved crop management within protected environments

Example farmer challenge

How might a farm extend their seasonal production window without negatively impacting profitability through cost of production?

03. Sustainable and Organic Practices

Strengthen soil, crop and resource health

Ideas that improve the long-term sustainability of farm operations while supporting crop health, input efficiency and responsible resource use.

Examples may include:

-   Soil-health improvement

-   Compost and organic soil amendments

-   Biological crop inputs

-   Integrated pest and disease management

-   Reduction of chemical inputs

-   Nutrient-use efficiency

-   Agricultural-waste reuse

-   Circular farming practices

-   Climate-resilient crops or production methods

Example farmer challenge

How might a farm improve soil quality and crop health while reducing dependency on conventional inputs?

04. Smart Farming and Digital Technology

Make farm operations more precise, connected and efficient

Ideas that use data, sensing, automation or digital technologies to improve decisions, reduce labour requirements and strengthen operational control.

Examples may include:

-   Sensors and remote monitoring

-   Farm-management platforms

-   Artificial intelligence and predictive analytics

-   Robotics and automation

-   Drones and imaging technologies

-   Early detection of crop stress, pests or disease

-   Digital traceability and record management

-   Yield forecasting

-   Equipment and resource monitoring

Example farmer challenge

How might a farm use real-time data to identify problems earlier and make better operational decisions?

6. How the Fund Works

Eyebrow

THE PARTICIPANT JOURNEY

From initial idea to a funded project

The Farm Innovation Fund supports participants through two connected journeys.

First, applicants develop and present a strong project proposal through workshops, mentoring and expert evaluation. Selected projects then move through detailed scoping, contracting, funded implementation and results review.

Journey 1: From Application to Selection

01. Submit an Initial Application

Tell us about the challenge and your idea

Applicants submit a concise initial application explaining:

-   The agricultural or on-farm challenge

-   Why the challenge matters

-   The proposed idea or potential solution

-   The expected benefit

-   The applicant or idea sponsor

Applications are reviewed for completeness and eligibility. Eligible applicants progress to the proposal-development stage.

02. Develop the Idea

Strengthen the problem, solution and project approach

Eligible applicants receive capability-building support to help turn the initial idea into a stronger and more deliverable project proposal.

Support may include:

-   Innovation workshops

-   Visits to IO’s facilities

-   Problem-definition exercises

-   Solution exploration

-   Technical and expert mentoring

-   Pilot-design guidance

-   Budget development

-   Success-measure definition

-   Pitch preparation

This stage helps participants understand the root problem, compare possible solutions and identify the evidence needed to determine whether an idea works.

What applicants develop

By the end of this stage, applicants should have:

-   A clearly defined agricultural challenge

-   An appropriate proposed solution

-   A realistic testing approach

-   An indicative project scope and budget

-   Defined technical, operational and commercial outcomes

-   An initial delivery team

03. Submit the Detailed Proposal

Translate the idea into a credible project

Participants submit a more detailed proposal setting out:

-   The challenge and current baseline

-   The proposed solution

-   Intended users or beneficiaries

-   Testing approach

-   Proposed location or testing environment

-   Required expertise and delivery partners

-   Project activities and timeline

-   Budget and resource requirements

-   Key risks and dependencies

-   Measures of success

-   Potential value beyond the initial pilot

Where a testing location has not yet been confirmed, applicants should describe the type of environment required. Suitable projects may later be matched with IO facilities, a participating farm or another relevant testing environment.

04. Expert Evaluation

Assess the project’s value and feasibility

Detailed proposals are reviewed by specialists with relevant agricultural, scientific, technical, operational and commercial expertise.

Evaluation considers:

-   Importance and clarity of the challenge

-   Relevance of the proposed solution

-   Potential to improve productivity, profitability or resilience at scale

-   Technical and operational feasibility

-   Quality of the proposed testing approach

-   Reasonableness of the budget

-   Applicant and delivery-team capability

-   Potential for farm adoption, replication, knowledge generation or commercialization

Applicants may receive questions or requests for clarification during this stage.

05. Finalist Pitch and Selection

Present the complete project proposal

Shortlisted applicants are invited to present their proposals to a panel.

Following the pitches, the panel selects the projects recommended to progress toward funding and implementation.

Selection is competitive. Progression to the finalist stage does not guarantee funding.

Selection outcome

Applicants are notified whether their project has been selected for implementation.

Journey 2: From Selection to Project Implementation

06. Project Readiness and Mobilization

Turn into a delivery-ready scope of work

The readiness process confirms:

-   Final project scope

-   Testing environment

-   Technical approach

-   Baseline and key performance indicators

-   Delivery activities

-   Project timeline

-   Approved budget

-   Procurement and supplier requirements

-   Roles and responsibilities

-   Data collection and reporting requirements

-   Project risks and controls

Before testing begins, the project team mobilizes the agreed resources and confirms that the delivery environment is ready.

07. Project Delivery and Measurement

Test the solution in relevant conditions

The project is implemented according to the agreed scope, timeline and milestones.

IO may support delivery through:

-   Project-management oversight

-   Scientific and technical expertise

-   Laboratory analysis

-   Greenhouse, nethouse or field facilities

-   Site visits

-   Partner coordination

-   Data collection and review

-   Risk and issue management

-   Budget monitoring

-   Milestone assessments

-   Technical troubleshooting

What may be measured

Measures will vary by project but could include:

-   Crop yield

-   Crop quality

-   Water consumption

-   Energy consumption

-   Input use

-   Labour requirements

-   Operating costs

-   Waste reduction

-   Resource recovery

-   Production reliability

-   Farm profitability

-   User or operator feedback

The objective is to generate credible evidence, not simply to demonstrate that the project was completed.

08. Project Review and Close-Out

Understand what worked and what should happen next

At the end of the pilot, the project team reviews the results against the agreed baseline, milestones and success criteria.

The close-out process may include:

-   Final technical and financial reporting

-   Performance-data analysis

-   Farmer and user feedback

-   Review of implementation challenges

-   Assessment of the business or adoption case

-   Documentation of knowledge generated

-   Intellectual-property review

-   Final project presentation

-   Farmer or ecosystem demonstration

Outcome

A clear evidence-based recommendation to:

-   Adopt the solution

-   Refine and test it further

-   Replicate it in another environment

-   Progress it toward commercialization

-   Conclude the project without further investment

A project that does not meet its original target may still produce valuable evidence about technical constraints, operating requirements or the conditions required for success.

09. Post-Project Scaling

Use the evidence to identify the next opportunity

Successful projects may progress through one or more pathways:

Replication

The solution is tested or adopted across additional farms, crops or operating environments.

Further development

The solution undergoes refinement, additional testing or a larger demonstration.

Local knowledge and intellectual property

The project generates locally relevant methods, data, technical know-how or potentially protectable intellectual property.

Commercial partnership

The project progresses toward procurement, licensing, manufacturing, distribution or another commercial arrangement.

New business opportunity

The applicant or project team develops a new product, service or farm-based enterprise.

Follow-on funding

The project attracts additional grant funding, investment, customer funding or partner contributions.

Progression beyond the funded pilot is not automatic. It depends on the evidence generated, market or farmer demand, ownership arrangements, team capability and the resources required for wider deployment.

National expertise supporting ideas from evaluation to scale

Partners contribute complementary expertise to help assess ideas, strengthen project design, support implementation and identify pathways for successful solutions to be adopted, replicated or developed further.

The role of each organization may vary according to the project and stage of the Fund, from expert evaluation and technical guidance to governance, validation, ecosystem connections and wider deployment support.

Ministry of Climate Change and Environment

8. How Projects Are Evaluated

Eyebrow

SELECTION

What makes a strong proposal?

Introductory copy

Projects are assessed by experts with relevant agricultural, scientific, technical, operational and commercial experience.

The evaluation considers both the proposed solution and the applicant’s readiness to deliver a structured on-farm pilot.

Evaluation areas

A real and important farm challenge

The proposal addresses a clearly defined need with a meaningful impact on farm operations.

A relevant solution

The proposed approach is connected to the problem and provides a clear improvement over current practice.

Practical feasibility

The solution can reasonably be implemented and tested within the available farm environment, budget and timeline.

Measurable value

The project has defined outcomes related to productivity, profitability, quality, resource use or resilience.

Delivery readiness

The required site, team, technical partners, approvals and operational resources can be mobilized.

Potential beyond the pilot

The project has the potential to generate useful knowledge, farm adoption, wider replication or a commercial opportunity.

Supporting note

Selection is competitive. Meeting the eligibility criteria does not guarantee funding.

Design guidance

-   Avoid long paragraphs or a scorecard-style table on the main page.

-   Provide detailed weightings and assessment rules in a downloadable applicant guide.

11. From Pilot to Longer-Term Value

Eyebrow

WHAT SUCCESS CAN UNLOCK

Turning farm learning into wider opportunity

Introductory copy

A completed pilot is not the final objective. Successful projects should create evidence and knowledge that helps determine what should happen next.

Not every project will follow the same route, but potential pathways include:

Outcome 1: Farm adoption

The participating farm continues using the solution after the funded pilot because it has demonstrated practical value.

Outcome 2: Replication and scale

The solution is implemented on additional farms, crops, production systems or operating sites.

Outcome 3: Local knowledge

The project generates data, methods, protocols or operating insights relevant to UAE farming conditions.

Outcome 4: Intellectual property

The project creates or strengthens a potentially protectable method, design, product or technical solution.

Outcome 5: New business opportunities

The team develops a new product, service, licensing model, farm-based business or commercial partnership.

Outcome 6: Continued development

The project progresses to a larger pilot, follow-on funding, commercial validation or another IO platform.

Important note

These are potential pathways rather than guaranteed outcomes. Progression depends on the project results, ownership arrangements, market demand and the capacity of the project team.

Design guidance

Use a branching pathway rather than a single linear arrow:

Funded pilot → Evidence generated → Multiple possible outcomes

Branches:

-   Adopt

-   Replicate

-   Protect

-   Commercialize

-   Develop further

This avoids suggesting every project will achieve commercialization.

15. Frequently Asked Questions

Recommended questions

Who is eligible to apply?

State the local applicant or sponsorship requirement clearly and concisely.

Can farm managers, researchers or technology companies participate?

Yes, as project partners where the application has an eligible local applicant or sponsor and a participating UAE farm.

Do I need a finished solution?

No. Applicants should have a clear farm challenge and an initial idea, but the Fund can help strengthen the proposed approach and testing plan.

How much funding is available?

Explain the potential maximum, award variability, eligible costs and milestone-based release.

Can I apply for standard equipment?

Clarify that equipment may only be eligible where it forms part of a genuine innovation project and an approved testing plan.

Who owns the intellectual property?

Provide a plain-language summary and link to the formal application terms.

What project costs are eligible?

Give representative examples and major exclusions.

How long will a funded project last?

Provide a typical range only once confirmed.

What reporting is required?

Explain milestone, budget, data and final-evaluation requirements.

What happens after the pilot?

Describe potential adoption, replication, further development and commercial pathways without guaranteeing progression.

Is support available in Arabic and English?

Confirm the delivery model before publication.

Design guidance

-   Use an accordion treatment.

-   Place downloadable documents alongside the FAQ:

    -   Applicant guide

    -   Eligibility guidance

    -   Evaluation criteria

    -   Eligible-cost guidance

    -   Terms and conditions

-   Provide Arabic and English versions wherever possible.

2. Program Proposition

Eyebrow

FARMERS KNOW THEIR FARMS

Enabling farmers to improve performance and profitability through on-farm innovation

Introductory copy

Farmers experience operational challenges directly and often have valuable ideas for solving them. However, testing a new approach can require funding, technical expertise, structured planning and a willingness to accept implementation risk.

The Farm Innovation Fund helps farmers turn practical ideas into defined solution and validation projects, supported by expert guidance, clear success measures and an evidence-based delivery process.

Three proposition columns

Farmer-led

The challenge and proposed solution originate from the farm’s own operating experience.

Practical

Projects should address a defined need and have a realistic pathway to implementation on a working farm.

Evidence-based

Each funded project is measured against agreed technical, operational and economic outcomes.

Recommended callout

This is not a general farm subsidy or equipment-purchasing scheme. Funding is intended to support structured innovation projects that test a new or meaningfully improved solution against a clearly defined farm challenge.

Audience groups

UAE farmers and farm owners

-   Demonstrate that the proposed idea is new to the relevant farm or meaningfully improves current practice

Content recommendation

The current heading“Open to farmers across the UAE, whatever the maturity of the idea” is directionally good, but potentially too broad.

Replace it with:

Open to farmer-led ideas at different stages of development

Then clarify:

Applicants do not need a fully developed technical proposal. However, they should be able to explain the problem, the proposed improvement and how the idea could be tested on their farm.

This preserves accessibility without implying that undefined concepts are automatically eligible.

5. How the Fund Works

Eyebrow

THE PROCESS

From farm need to field evidence

Introductory copy

The Fund follows a structured process designed to strengthen farmer-led ideas, identify projects with the greatest potential and support successful applicants through implementation.

Stage 1: Discovery and call

The Fund defines priority themes, eligibility, available support and application requirements before opening a new cycle.

Applicant action

Register for updates and review the eligibility guidance.

Stage 2: Initial application

Farmers submit a short application describing:

-   The farm and production system

-   The challenge or opportunity

-   The proposed idea

-   The expected benefit

-   The location where the project could be tested

Applicant action

Focus on the problem and practical opportunity. A complete technical proposal is not required at this stage.

Stage 3: Eligibility and initial review

Applications are checked for eligibility, strategic relevance and basic feasibility.

Review considerations

-   Clear farmer need

-   Potential farm-level value

-   Practicality

-   Applicant commitment

-   Potential to generate measurable evidence

Stage 4: Training and proposal development

Eligible applicants may be invited to workshops, facility visits or mentoring sessions to strengthen their project design.

Support may include

-   Problem definition

-   Project design (POC, prototype development, pilot)

-   Budget development

-   Success measures

-   Risk identification

-   Pitch preparation

Stage 5: Expert review and pitches

Detailed proposals are assessed by relevant scientific, agricultural, technical and commercial experts.

Shortlisted applicants may be invited to present their projects and respond to evaluator questions.

Evaluation areas

-   Importance of the farm challenge

-   Strength of the proposed solution

-   Technical feasibility

-   Implementation readiness

-   Expected agricultural or commercial benefit

-   Strength of the measurement plan

-   Potential for wider relevance

Stage 6: Project scoping and contracting

Selected projects are developed into an agreed delivery plan.

The plan should define

-   Project scope

-   Roles and responsibilities

-   Budget

-   Timeline

-   Milestones

-   Data requirements

-   Success measures

-   Payment or reimbursement terms

-   Reporting expectations

-   IP, confidentiality and communications arrangements

Stage 7: Project delivery

The farmer, IO and relevant delivery partners implement the agreed project in real operating conditions.

Progress is monitored through regular reporting, site visits and milestone reviews.

Stage 8: Results and next pathway

Results are evaluated against the agreed baseline and success criteria.

Successful projects may progress toward:

-   Continued use on the participating farm

-   Replication across other farms

-   Partnership with a technology provider

-   Follow-on funding

-   Commercial deployment

-   Wider demonstration or knowledge sharing

Design feedback

The current five-step timeline is visually effective but compresses several materially different activities.

The displayed journey appears to combine:

-   Discovery

-   Eligibility

-   Training

-   Review

-   Test and scale

I recommend using six visible stages on the page and placing project scoping and contracting within the selected-project section:

1.  Apply

2.  Eligibility review

3.  Training and proposal development

4.  Expert review and pitches

5.  Project design and contracting

6.  Project delivery and results

This remains easy to understand while more accurately representing the operational process.
