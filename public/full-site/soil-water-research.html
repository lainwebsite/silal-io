Soil & Water Research

Improving the foundations
of desert agriculture

iO develops practical solutions that improve soil health, increase agricultural water productivity and strengthen crop performance under arid and saline conditions.

Request analysis See the research areas
The challenge

Every crop begins with the condition of its soil and water

UAE agriculture faces a combination of extreme heat, scarce and increasingly saline water, and sandy soils with limited capacity to retain water, nutrients and organic matter. These constraints reduce input efficiency, affect crop performance and increase the cost and complexity of local production.

Key constraints
  1. 01

    Low Water and Nutrient Retention

    Sandy soils can lose water and nutrients quickly, reducing the efficiency of irrigation and fertilizer applications.

  2. 02

    Salinity

    Saline irrigation water and salt accumulation in the root zone can restrict nutrient uptake, affect plant growth and reduce long-term soil productivity.

  3. 03

    Low Organic Matter and Biological Activity

    Limited organic matter and microbiome diversity can affect soil structure, fertility, nutrient cycling and resilience.

  4. 04

    High Input Requirements

    When the condition of the soil and water is not fully understood, farms may apply more water, fertilizer and amendments than the crop requires.

Using every litre more effectively

Improving agricultural water productivity

iO researches how water quality, irrigation design, crop demand and soil condition interact to affect agricultural performance. The goal is not simply to reduce irrigation volume. It is to deliver the right amount and quality of water at the right time while protecting crop performance and long-term soil health.

Flagship programme

From laboratory result to crop-specific instruction

The Soil & Water Health Programme combines farm sampling, laboratory analysis and agronomic interpretation to help growers understand what their soil and irrigation water require.

The programme currently operates across the Abu Dhabi region, Al Dhafra and the Northern Emirates, with field engineers and mobile capability bringing sampling and guidance closer to the farm.

  1. 105Reports delivered with written recommendations
  2. 89Farm holdings covered by the sampling campaign
  3. 192Soil and irrigation-water analyses
  4. 13Field engineers carrying it to the grower

Soil Sampling Campaign portfolio, 2025 to 2026. Figures to be labelled by reporting period and updated as the programme progresses.

How the programme works
  1. Step 1

    Sample the Farm

    • Field engineers collect soil and irrigation-water samples using controlled procedures. Crop type, production system, management history and field context are recorded.
  2. Step 2

    Analyze in the Laboratory

    • Soil, irrigation water and plant tissue are assessed using relevant laboratory methods and instrumentation: ICP-OES, CHNS analysis, chromatography and controlled laboratory methods.
  3. Step 3

    Interpret for UAE Conditions

    • Results are considered against the characteristics of local soils, irrigation water and production systems. Cranfield University supports the development of thresholds, reference values and methodological assurance, while the sampling, laboratory analysis, agronomic interpretation and farm recommendations remain led by Silal.
  4. Step 4

    Recommend by Crop

    • A written plan converts the results into practical guidance tailored to the crop, soil and water: fertilizer rate, nutrient form, application frequency, fertigation timing, soil amendments, salinity management, and practices that should be reduced or discontinued.
  5. Step 5

    Verify on the Crop

    • Follow-up soil, leaf or water analysis determines whether the intervention delivered the expected change. Advice can then be refined based on evidence rather than repeated from previous seasons.

UAE-relevant interpretation

Values are interpreted in the context of calcareous sandy soils and arid agricultural conditions rather than relying only on reference values developed for temperate farming systems.

Farm-specific action

Recommendations can specify:

  • What to apply
  • How much to apply
  • Which form to use
  • How often to apply it
  • What should be reduced or stopped
  • Which result should be monitored to verify improvement

Follow-up evidence

Soil and plant re-analysis can confirm whether the recommendation delivered the intended result and whether the plan should be adjusted.

Research in action

Using evidence to reduce unnecessary inputs

A site-specific fertilizer review at a Silal greenhouse compared actual soil and water conditions with the existing nutrient-management program.

The analysis identified nutrient applications that were not aligned with crop requirements and informed a more targeted fertigation plan.

  • 19%Approximate reduction in fertilizer cost
  • AED 3,000Approximately saved per greenhouse per month
  • 30–40%Approximate reduction in nitrate leaching
Other reported site-specific outcomes
  • Optimized fertilizer-use efficiency
  • Balanced nutrient supply
  • Reduced soil-salinization risk
  • Improved soil-fertility and nutrient balance

Outcomes are site-specific, as recorded in the programme material, and need to be re-verified for each farm.

Building a UAE standard

Soil-health thresholds developed for desert farming systems

iO and Cranfield University are working to develop UAE Soil Health Metrics and a Soil Health Scorecard.

The twelve-month project, initiated in May 2026, is intended to provide a more consistent framework for sampling, interpreting and comparing soil condition under UAE agricultural conditions.

The development pathway
01

Review sampling methods, quality criteria, metadata and the data dictionary.

Define the indicators, thresholds and scoring framework relevant to UAE soils.

Test whether the scorecard ranks farm conditions appropriately against existing and newly generated datasets.

Transfer the method to field engineers, growers and relevant stakeholders through workshops and guidance.

  • 01

    Methodology and Data Structure

    Review sampling methods, quality criteria, metadata and the data dictionary.

  • 02

    Metrics and Scoring Logic

    Define the indicators, thresholds and scoring framework relevant to UAE soils.

  • 03

    Validation

    Test whether the scorecard ranks farm conditions appropriately against existing and newly generated datasets.

  • 04

    Training and Dissemination

    Transfer the method to field engineers, growers and relevant stakeholders through workshops and guidance.

Why a UAE scorecard is needed
  • Define relevant soil-health indicators
  • Establish UAE reference values and thresholds
  • Compare farms and fields consistently
  • Track improvement over time
  • Strengthen the scientific basis of recommendations
  • Build a structured national soil dataset
  • Support common understanding among growers, advisers and policymakers

Intended output: a score from 0 to 100 is proposed to provide a clearer, consistent view of soil condition against UAE-calibrated thresholds. The scorecard remains under development.

Scientific foundation: the project also benefits from access to archived UAE soil-survey material dating from 1961 to 1994, including survey work associated with the Al Ain oases, which helps place current soil conditions in a longer-term context.
